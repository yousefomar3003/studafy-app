# Capacity: `study_lesson`, 17 October 2026

Item 2 of the "Next" list in the [16 October report](2026-10-16.md): the lesson
the AI study tools and study notebooks read, `private.study_lesson`. It is
proven to change no answer, then measured with the same harnesses, on top of
fixes 1–6 and the notebook queue (#78).

## The short answer

**A `study_lesson` call is now about 15–25 times cheaper in the database when
the machine is quiet, and 3–5 times cheaper under load, with the same answers.**
Its share of database time falls from about 3.5–4.5% to under 1%. On the same
machine and the same synthetic data, before and after:

|                                                               | Before                    | After                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Per call, inside the database (2,400 calls, nothing else run) | 3.2–3.5 ms (p50 2.7–3.0)  | 0.12–0.21 ms (p50 0.11–0.16) |
| Per call, from a client, round trip included (1,200 calls)    | 4.3–5.2 ms (p50 4.0–4.2)  | 0.6–1.4 ms (p50 0.6–0.9)     |
| Through the API under load: share of database time            | 3.8–4.4%, 19–30 ms a call | 0.7–0.9%, 5.7–6.6 ms a call  |
| Database alone (`bench.ts`, full mix): share of database time | 3.4%, 40 ms a call        | 0.3%, 1.1 ms a call          |

End-to-end throughput and latency did **not** resolve the change: the machine
was far more loaded than on 16 October (load average 85–350 on 10 cores, from
other sessions' iOS simulator builds and Supabase stacks), and runs of the same
code differed from each other by more than before differed from after. The
throughput tables below are given for completeness only. The per-call and
share-of-time figures are the reliable ones: they compare the same calls,
interleaved, and the share is a ratio within one run.

The gain is real but bounded: `study_lesson` was at most about 4–5% of database
time, so taking it to under 1% frees roughly that much capacity, not more.

Every answer was compared with the original for every sampled person and lesson
on the scale data, with edge cases planted, and for everyone on the pgTAP
fixtures: **zero differences**.

## What changed

| Migration                                       | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `202610160010_capacity_study_lesson_access.sql` | `study_lesson` picked the newest publication of a lesson that the caller may see, asking `can_view_resource_publication` of each. That function is a chain of SQL functions that cannot be inlined (each is `SECURITY DEFINER` with its own `search_path`): `notebook_reader_allowed`, `is_school_admin` and `has_active_membership` (each with `is_active_user`), then `is_enrolled_student` and `is_verified_guardian` for the caller's student records and links (each with `is_active_user` and `has_active_membership` again). In PostgreSQL 17 a SQL function's queries are planned again on every top-level call, so each request planned and ran about ten small queries for one decision. `study_lesson` is now plpgsql (its plans are kept for the life of the connection) and decides access itself, once per call: it finds the caller's facts first — an active user; the schools where they are school admin or teacher; the classes they are enrolled in; the classes of children they are a verified guardian of — each by its own index from the caller's id, then picks the newest allowed publication from the same candidates in the same order |

No rule changed. Restated for this path (the publication already published, not
withdrawn, for a class, and of the current version of a published, live lesson),
the rule is: an active user who either holds an active school admin or teacher
membership in the publication's school, or — when the version's file, if any, is
clean and not deleted — is enrolled in the class (students or both) or is a
verified guardian of a child enrolled in it (guardians or both), with the same
enrolment, class, term, link-expiry and membership conditions as
`is_enrolled_student` and `is_verified_guardian`. The old rule's branches for
publications without a class are not restated, because `study_lesson` never
considers those.

The school-admin branch is the existing rule, kept so that the decisions are
identical: it gives school admins nothing they did not have. The new function
does not call `is_school_admin`, `is_class_staff` or `api041_class_writer`; it
reads the membership directly, as `has_active_membership` did. No other function
was restated. `can_view_resource_publication` itself is unchanged and still
serves the row-level policies and the other readers. No function is renamed or
wrapped (ADR-0030), and no index was needed.

A first version wrote the rule into the lesson query as correlated subqueries.
It was slower than the original (12 ms of planning per call, and a poor join
order for students), which is why the caller's sets are found first, each a
small indexed query.

## Proof: zero differences

The method of fixes 1–6 (`scripts/scale/proof/`, see its README): in one
transaction, plant the edge cases, record every answer, apply the migration,
record again, compare, roll back. On the pgTAP fixtures, which already had the
migration, the original function (its definition before the migration, from
`pg_get_functiondef`) is restored first in the same transaction, so "before"
runs exactly the code being replaced.

`study-lesson-differential.sql` with `plant-study-lesson.sql`: for every sampled
person, every lesson published (in any state) to a class they are related to in
any way (taught, enrolled, a linked child's, current or not), a share of the
lessons of every school they have any membership in, five lessons of schools
they have nothing to do with, and signed-out and unknown callers; the whole
answer (lesson, version, class, title, body, or null) compared.

| Data                                                                         | People | Calls  | Allowed | Differences |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------- | ----------- |
| Scale, 1 in 500 people and the planted admins, 1 lesson in 10 of each school | 234    | 47,557 | 12,747  | **0**       |
| pgTAP fixtures, everyone, every lesson                                       | 24     | 530    | 82      | **0**       |

The seed has one publication per lesson, published to students and guardians, no
files on versions and no parent memberships (so no guardian could see a lesson
at all), so the plant adds, on top of the lesson extras of fix 4
(`plant-lessons.sql`): versions with a file being scanned, rejected, clean and
deleted, and clean; publications to students only and to guardians only; a
withdrawn publication with an older live one to another class; a second
publication to another class at the same moment (a tie) and a newer one to a
third; a draft publication and a published one with no class; and, on people:
parent or guardian memberships for most guardians, none for a tenth (half of
those holding one in another school instead); a school admin per school who
teaches elsewhere and one who is a parent elsewhere; inactive, withdrawn, ended
and future enrolments; expired, soon-expiring and pending links; lapsed, future
and suspended memberships; archived classes; closed terms; suspended and deleted
profiles; deleted student records; a suspended and a deleted school. On the
scale data the 47,557 calls included 5,900 allowed to school staff (3,378 of
them school admins of a school they do not teach in), 3,779 allowed as a
student, 3,068 as a guardian, 32,043 refused to students and guardians, 8,458 on
versions with a file (1,105 allowed), 2,111 on lessons with several live
publications and 3,824 published to one audience.

**The proof catches mistakes.** It was run against eight deliberately broken
versions of the new function. Every one showed differences:

| Broken version                        | Scale (calls differing) | Fixtures |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------- |
| School admins dropped (teachers only) | 3,074                   | 20       |
| Audience ignored for students         | 137                     | 0\*      |
| Version's file not checked            | 683                     | 12       |
| Guardian membership in any school     | 198                     | 0\*      |
| Inactive users allowed                | 513                     | 10       |
| Guardian link expiry ignored          | 144                     | 0\*      |
| Oldest publication first              | 256                     | 0\*      |
| Closed terms ignored for students     | 250                     | 0\*      |

The real migration shows none, in the same runs. \* The pgTAP fixtures are small
(24 people, 530 calls): they hold no lesson with two live publications, no
guardian-only publication a student could reach, and no expired link or closed
term on a path that would otherwise be allowed, so those mistakes cannot show
there. The scale run, where all eight show, is the one that proves the proof.

All pgTAP files pass in CI order on a fresh reset of a throwaway stack with the
migration, and so do the database lint, the query-plan checks (the coursework
one hit its 100 ms latency bound once while the machine's load average was over
100, and passed on rerun), the database type drift check and the API and worker
integration suites. The DB-020 and DB-021 upgrade suites (which need
`scripts/rehearse-recovery.ts`), the Storage end-to-end drills, the moderation
drill, the outbox end-to-end suite and the AUTH-030 lifecycle script were not
run: they do not touch this function, and they default to the developer's own
stack.

## Measurements

**Environment.** As on 16 October: the Supabase local stack (Postgres 17) in a
Docker VM with 10 vCPUs and 8 GB, `scripts/scale/seed-scale.sql` at 100 schools
× 500 students, brought up to `main` (migrations through `202610160001`), one
Bun API process (`DATABASE_POOL_MAX=20`, Redis, rate limits on), the local AI
and sign-in stand-ins, a warm-up first. Runs were interleaved: before, after,
before again (the original function restored), after again.

The machine was much busier than on 16 October, from other sessions (iOS
simulator builds, several Supabase stacks): the load average was 85–350 on 10
cores during the throughput runs. Compare only within this report.

**Per call.** The same 1,200 (person, lesson) pairs: 600 students and a lesson
of one of their classes, 200 guardians and a lesson of a child's class (all

## Rerunning it

As in the [12 October report](2026-10-12.md#rerunning-it), plus:

```sh
# The proof (about 20 minutes on the scale data, most of it the plant):
psql "$DB" -X -v plant=scripts/scale/proof/plant-study-lesson.sql \
  -v migration=supabase/migrations/202610160010_capacity_study_lesson_access.sql \
  -v sample=500 -f scripts/scale/proof/study-lesson-differential.sql
# On the pgTAP fixtures (which already have the migration): restore the
# original first, and plant on every lesson and person:
psql "$DB" -X -v pre1=<the original study_lesson definition> \
  -v plant=scripts/scale/proof/plant-study-lesson.sql -v seed_lessons=1 \
  -v lesson_every=1 -v access_every=1 -v resource_every=1 -v sample=1 \
  -v migration=supabase/migrations/202610160010_capacity_study_lesson_access.sql \
  -f scripts/scale/proof/study-lesson-differential.sql
```
